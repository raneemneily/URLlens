URLens · research prototype

See why a link looks dangerous.

Most phishing filters give a verdict and nothing else. URLens scores a link in your browser and explains every signal behind the score.

Try:

Nothing you type leaves this page. Everything runs locally in your browser.

How it works

1. Parse

The URL is split into scheme, userinfo, host, path and query, the same way a browser does it.

2. Check

Twelve lightweight rules look for known phishing tricks. Each has a weight based on how reliable it is.

3. Explain

The weights add up to a score from 0 to 100, and every triggered rule says why it matters.

Signals we check

Brand typosquatDomain is within 2 edits of a known brand, or uses 0/1/3 swaps (paypa1, micr0soft)+35
Brand in wrong placeBrand name appears in a subdomain of a different domain+30
PunycodeHost starts with xn--, often used for lookalike characters+30
@ in URLEverything before the @ is ignored by browsers, so it hides the real host+25
IP as hostLegitimate services almost never ask you to log in on a raw IP+25
Cheap TLD.xyz, .top, .zip and similar are over-represented in abuse reports+15
Credential keywordslogin, verify, secure, update, account in host or path+10 to +15
OtherNo HTTPS, too many subdomains, very long URL, hyphen-heavy host+5 to +10

Limits and next steps

URLens is a heuristic prototype. It can miss a clean-looking phishing link on a compromised legitimate site, and it can flag a harmless link that looks unusual. It reads the text of a URL and never visits it.

  • Measure precision and recall on a public labeled dataset such as PhishTank or OpenPhish feeds.
  • Replace hand-set weights with a small logistic regression trained on that data, keeping the explanations.
  • Add homoglyph detection beyond punycode, and a Tunisian brand list (banks, Poste, telecoms).
  • Ship it as a browser extension that warns before the page loads.
